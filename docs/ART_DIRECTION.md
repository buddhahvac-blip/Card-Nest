# CardNest Art Direction — Locked Visual Standard

Founder direction: the current high-quality CardNest Common masters and current Rare / Epic / Legendary showcase cards are the primary visual reference for all future card artwork.

The goal is a premium digital TCG that feels clean, collectible, cinematic, original, and readable on mobile.

## Primary reference set

Use the current production art direction established by:

- CN1-001–011 high-quality Common master presentation
- CN1-108 Deepstream Oarfish
- CN1-168 Orchidhelm Guardian
- CN1-229 Roadwarden Caracara
- CN1-299 Constellation Keeper Serpent
- CN1-307 Aurora Herald
- CN1-359 Velvet Coil Serpent
- CN1-068 Ember Sovereign where its current Legendary presentation remains useful

Do not use older blurry, low-resolution, baked-card raster assets as the quality target.

## Core visual DNA

Future CardNest creatures should be:

- original fantasy creatures with strong, readable silhouettes
- family-friendly without looking preschool-oriented
- cute, beautiful, majestic, mysterious, or powerful depending on Theme
- polished enough to feel like premium collectible art
- full of personality in the face and pose
- readable at thumbnail size
- shown in a meaningful habitat, not floating on a generic background
- lit cinematically with clear subject/background separation
- detailed without becoming visually cluttered
- clean enough that the creature remains the visual focal point

## CardNest world look

The world should feel:

- magical but grounded
- lush, cinematic, and painterly
- deep teal / blue-green / metallic-gold compatible
- warm and inviting even when the Theme is dark
- rich in environmental storytelling
- consistent enough that all six Themes clearly belong to the same universe

Theme identity should come from habitat, light, material, motion, and creature design — not just changing the color palette.

## Theme direction

### Ember
Volcanic warmth, basalt, ember glow, sun-warmed stone, firelight, ash, enduring strength.

### Tide
Water, ruins, refracted light, flowing forms, aquatic depth, calm movement, ancient sanctuaries.

### Bloom
Living forests, flowers, meadows, roots, sunlight, softness, growth, warmth, playful life.

### Volt
Wind, altitude, storms, open air, movement, distant electricity, speed, weathered paths.

### Mystic
Auroras, stars, celestial architecture, luminous paths, wonder, ancient scale, quiet magic.

### Shadow
Moonlight, mist, velvet textures, indigo valleys, mystery, softness, quiet tension — never gore or horror.

## Rarity changes the experience, not image quality

Every rarity must be professionally rendered.

### Common
- clean, beautiful, readable
- strong personality
- clear habitat
- simpler presentation
- no disposable filler feeling

### Rare
- richer environmental storytelling
- more atmosphere and specificity
- stronger scene composition

### Epic
- cinematic scale or intimacy
- deeper environmental storytelling
- more dramatic light and motion potential

### Legendary
- unforgettable world-level scene
- strongest storytelling
- strongest silhouette
- premium presentation / Living World potential
- may receive subtle motion or special viewing treatment
- should not automatically receive stronger gameplay stats

## Composition rules

Prefer:

- one clear primary creature
- full-body or nearly full-body pose when practical
- strong readable silhouette
- foreground / subject / background depth
- eye direction and pose that imply personality
- habitat elements that support lore
- composition that survives mobile crop and thumbnail display

Avoid:

- repeated generic front-facing poses
- excessive particles
- excessive gold
- empty backgrounds
- over-busy framing
- extreme bloom/glow that destroys detail
- generic “AI fantasy” clutter
- obvious resemblance to third-party characters or franchises

## Source-art rules

Raw master artwork should contain:

- no card number
- no rarity text
- no stats
- no logo
- no CardNest wordmark
- no watermark
- no baked-in card frame

Card text, numbers, stats, Theme, class, rarity, and UI belong in code whenever possible so they remain crisp and correct.

## Resolution and quality

Do not enlarge weak source art and call it high resolution.

For premium source art:

- use genuine native detail
- preserve clean edges and texture
- avoid over-sharpening halos
- avoid muddy compression
- prefer production masters that comfortably support the intended display size
- keep optimized web derivatives separate from source-quality masters

A card should not be prominently showcased if its source does not support the display size.

## Digital TCG presentation

CardNest should feel designed for digital collecting first.

Prioritize:

- sharp mobile rendering
- clean responsive layouts
- high-DPI artwork
- fast loading
- tap-to-inspect
- artwork-only viewing when useful
- subtle optional motion for premium rarities
- visual consistency across collection, card page, My Nest, and pack reveal

## Originality rule

Competitor research may influence principles such as clarity, rarity hierarchy, collection excitement, and animation restraint.

Never copy:

- another franchise's creature
- card frame
- logo
- proprietary symbols
- recognizable layout
- distinctive trade dress
- exact visual effects
- proprietary naming

CardNest must remain recognizably its own world.

## Future-generation rule

Before generating a new card, compare the brief to the current approved CardNest reference set.

A new piece should be rejected or revised if it:

- looks cheaper than the current Commons
- looks stylistically unrelated to the current set
- uses a generic or repetitive silhouette
- has weak habitat storytelling
- is blurry / low-detail at intended display size
- includes baked-in text or watermarks
- resembles third-party IP
- loses the clean premium digital-TCG feel

## Founder quality principle

**Common should look beautiful.  
Rare should feel special.  
Epic should feel cinematic.  
Legendary should feel unforgettable.**

Future artwork should strengthen this visual language rather than reinvent it each batch.
