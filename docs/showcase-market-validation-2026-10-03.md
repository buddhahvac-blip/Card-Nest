# CardNest — fans first, customers later

Research and implementation review: **3 October 2026**. Mission: **A World Worth Collecting**. Season One: **The First Flight**. Promise: **Collect Now. Play Later.**

The strongest opportunity is a guardian universe people want to revisit for its habitats and personalities. CardNest should validate that emotional pull before commissioning hundreds of images or launching commerce. No market-size, revenue, retention or unique-customer claim is made here.

## Market: verified mechanics and CardNest interpretation

Official sources describe mechanics, not proof that a particular mechanic caused commercial success. Match times below are marketing claims or design targets, not measured averages. Historical introduction posts are dated explicitly; they are not treated as current patch notes.

| Product | Strongest collecting mechanic | Strongest art idea | Strongest gameplay idea | Strongest community idea | CardNest lesson |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pokémon TCG Pocket [1,2] | Free pack ritual; binders and display boards | Immersive habitat art | Streamlined 20-card battles and assisted onboarding | Displaying favorite finds | Let one guardian open a world; retain an art-only path without requiring competition |
| Marvel Snap [3,4] | Variant albums with milestones | Alternate interpretation of the same character | Officially advertised 12-card decks and three-minute matches | Seasonal reveals and creator participation | Small decks reduce choices; CardNest needs its own sanctuary objective and album stories |
| Disney Lorcana [5] | Collecting favorite characters alongside play | Character and world continuity across treatments | Several entry formats | Weekly play, prereleases and championships | Give families recurring shared discoveries before pursuing tournaments |
| MTG Arena [6] | Wildcards and free/optional mastery tracks | Cosmetic sleeves, companions and avatars | Tutorial bot and starter decks | Events at several commitment levels | Teach with a fixed Common deck, not a paid collection requirement |
| Hearthstone [7,8] | Expansion reward tracks; free Core foundation | Premium cosmetics and distinct heroes | Accessible entry with long-term deck mastery | Cinematic and staged expansion reveals | Separate free gameplay foundations from optional appearance purchases |
| Legends of Runeterra [9] | Champion/adventure progression | World map and champion storytelling | Path of Champions PvE progression | Regular champion reveals | A later solo sanctuary journey can validate combinations before multiplayer |
| Shadowverse: Worlds Beyond [10] | Compendium progress and free daily pack | Card-style/background cosmetic bundles | Story and missions support card access | Event-linked collection progression | Make progress legible; avoid reward systems that require continual purchases |
| Slay the Spire 2 [11] | Run discoveries rather than a permanent pack storefront | Environments and character identity | Synergies learned through repeatable runs | Early Access feedback; up to four-player co-op | Test a small ruleset with real feedback before expanding it; this is a roguelike comparator |
| Flesh and Blood [12] | Event-related art treatments | Extended art and cosmetic chase presentation | Casual-to-competitive learning path | Weekly Armory and beginner social play | Host a small, welcoming art/play session instead of buying a tournament infrastructure |

**Continuity caution:** Altered announced that its marketplace would close on 30 April 2026 and digital collection services on 20 May 2026 [13]. It is not presented as an active success story. CardNest needs collection export/continuity planning before customers depend on a paid digital service.

**Community evidence is qualitative:** A July 2026 Snap album thread contains disagreement about buying variants for rewards versus buying artwork one actually likes [14]. Another asks for clearer missing-card tracking [15]. These small, self-selected discussions justify testing clarity and purchase pressure; they cannot establish market-wide preferences. Pocket immersive-art discussions similarly suggest distinct completion and favorite-character motives; no representative survey was performed.

### Art, access, retention and monetization implications

Strong silhouettes and one focal creature are useful at phone size; detailed habitats reward inspection. Alternate art should tell a new moment, not recolor the same pose. Motion should enrich the environment while preserving a static collectible image. The six showcase studies test ocean depth, botanical warmth, wind altitude, cosmic scale, velvet intimacy and aurora protection. More decoration is not a substitute for more story.

Daily pack rituals can make returning easy, but CardNest should first offer a gentle weekly reveal and an optional favorite trail. Weekly album/lore updates and seasonal habitat chapters are proposed. No streak loss, purchase countdown, scarce investment language or daily pressure is added.

Free starter play, clear abilities and cosmetic distinction are better foundations than rarity-based power. Existing products still mix card access and monetization in different ways; this report does not certify any of them as universally fair. Avoid paid power, confusing probabilities, mandatory paid album completion, child-targeted spending prompts, resale promises and support-heavy trading.

Future revenue experiments should begin with clearly described, fixed-content optional art/cosmetic bundles after rights, audience, fees and support review. A season pass, subscription, affiliate feed or physical/digital crossover needs a separate value proposition and approval. **No commerce, subscription, new partner link or live Stripe activation is part of this build.**

## CardNest differentiation and collection identity

A guardian belongs to a habitat, protects a passage and remembers safe journeys. A Nest expresses the collector's taste rather than a generic follower profile. The deterministic teal/gold frame keeps canon outside the image-generation process.

| Fan | First useful experience | Later hypothesis |
|---|---|---|
| Art collector | Clean portrait, close-up and Favorite | Founder-reviewed alternate habitat moments |
| Completion collector | Stable 369-card index, owned/missing status | Theme albums with cosmetic milestones |
| World fan | Habitat, lore, Living World story study | Connected sanctuary chapters |
| Casual player | Existing local practice sketch | Fixed free beginner deck |
| Strategy player | Canonical class/ability profiles | Small-deck combination tests |
| Showcase collector | Device-local shelves and Theme affinity | Private Nest decoration/export, later reviewed sharing |

Three implemented album studies are **The Bloom Garden** (12), **Paths Above the Storm** (12) and **Six First Horizons** (6). The first two include Commons. Saved interest never counts as ownership; duplicate copies do not inflate progress. My Nest derives labels such as Meadow Keeper, Skyway Guardian and Starfield Seeker from device Favorites. Ties use a deterministic order; this is not a psychological classification.

Proposed rewards: three unique guardians → badge; six → decoration; twelve or album completion → lore. These are **not awarded**. A future First Flight Helpers badge could recognize consented beta participation without monetary benefits; no financial founder token or investment promise is proposed. Public profiles, achievement claims and account complexity are deferred.

## Nest Battles specification — separate, unplaytested proposal

**Recommend testing 14 guardian cards**, two copies maximum, two active slots. A 10-card deck may become repetitive; an 18-card deck may obscure a new player's first combination. These are hypotheses, not simulated balance findings. Benchmark all three with the same starter roster. Target median 3–6 minutes after tutorial; do not advertise this as achieved.

Start with three cards in hand and one predeployed Common guardian. Draw one at turn start; hand cap six. Alternate turns, with one resource refresh each personal turn: two energy, then three, then four maximum; unused energy expires. Deployment costs canonical energy (usually three), so turn-one interaction uses the starting guardian. At most one basic pulse and one ability per turn. Newly deployed guardians cannot act until their next turn. Cooldowns use that guardian's personal turns. No paid advantage or collection requirement in the starter mode.

Prototype win condition: protect a 20-integrity Nest while reducing the opposing Nest to zero. A ready defending Warden/Vanguard must be targeted before Nest damage. To speed initial paper tests, normalize canonical HP/ATK/DEF/SPD by ten **in a separate battle adapter** without changing stored stats. Basic pulse costs one energy and uses normalized attack. Armor reduces guardian-target damage by floor(normalized defense / 2), minimum one; it does not reduce unguarded Nest damage. Guard/heal/reduce-speed abilities scale their canonical amount by ten. Guard absorbs damage before health; healing caps at maximum; defeated guardians leave active slots. Speed orders triggered effects only. A cap of eight rounds ends in higher remaining Nest integrity, then remaining guardian health, then draw. These draft rules need fairness and duration tests.

| Class | First understandable job | Balance concern |
|---|---|---|
| Scout | Act quickly, improve positioning | Speed must not multiply damage freely |
| Striker | Break an exposed defense | Burst must allow response |
| Vanguard | Hold a route | Defense plus damage can dominate |
| Warden | Protect another guardian | Guard/heal stalemates |
| Support | Restore and enable allies | Infinite sustain loops |
| Disruptor | Delay or weaken a plan | Permanent action denial |

Theme identities are **proposals**: Ember tempo, Tide timing, Bloom recovery, Volt mobility, Mystic protection/setup, Shadow information/disruption. No exclusive rare ability is required to learn these strategies. Rarity stays cosmetic; equal stat totals alone do not prove balanced abilities.

Example five-turn paper exchange, isolating an unguarded Nest to explain basic actions: each side has a ready 40-attack guardian (normalized pulse four). Your first five turns reduce the rival from 20 → 16 → 12 → 8 → 4 → 0. Rival answers after turns one through four, leaving your Nest at four. The example demonstrates an intentionally exposed race, not a recommended strategy or proof of first-player fairness. Repeat with defender guard deployment and swapped first player to find the obvious tempo imbalance before implementation.

Beginner deck proposal: two copies each of Leafpaw #002, Thorncap #008, Leafling #130, Twig Insect #131, Bloomstag #132, Orchid Mantis #133 and Bramble Hedgehog #134. Fourteen guardian cards; grant practice access independent of ownership. Review class coverage, energy curve and actual ability interactions before calling it balanced. The public site's existing three-action local sketch is unchanged; this ruleset, multiplayer, matchmaking and rewards are **not launched**.

## Fan flywheel and inexpensive reveal program

| Stage | CardNest implementation | Useful observation |
|---|---|---|
| Discover | Six-guardian showcase on landing | Showcase reach |
| Connect | Habitat, Theme and canonical guardian route | Guardian views |
| Collect | Favorite, Wishlist, My Nest; sample pack preview | Save actions and pack choice |
| Chase | Small narrative albums | Album exploration, real owned progress |
| Show | Existing share button | Share action, not verified recipient |
| Play | Optional local sketch | Battle entry after saving in same session |
| Return | Proposed weekly stories and seasonal chapters | Written reasons to return; later voluntary research |
| Learn | Founder-only Nest Mind aggregates | Suggest experiments; never auto-release |

Low-cost weekly reveal proposal: Monday silhouette, Tuesday habitat clue, Wednesday lore fragment, Thursday voluntary prediction, Friday clean full art/permanent card route. Reuse one approved illustration and accessible text. No paid campaign, public-chat moderation burden or automatic social posting. After validation, invite a few small creators to reveal one reviewed guardian each under explicit permissions and clear disclosures; no outreach has been sent.

Recruit **50–100 actual beta collectors** through founder invitations to willing adults/family facilitators and permitted community posts. That is a goal, not a reported count. Use `/beta`: discover → inspect → Favorite → Wishlist/album → pack preview → share by choice → My Nest → honest feedback. A ten-minute moderated session should ask what they remember, why they chose a guardian, what ownership means, which experience they would revisit and what confused them. Sessions are not people; consented research recruitment must track participants separately without linking anonymous analytics identities.

## Analytics and Nest Mind permissions

Implemented: most-viewed/favorited/wishlisted/shared guardians; Theme selection and card engagement; rarity presentation action totals; pack-preview mix; showcase, Living World and album reach; feedback priorities/intent; latest 20 founder-only written return reasons; battle-after-save receipt order within one anonymous session. Writes to session and daily aggregate tables are atomic. Invalid card/album IDs and extra payload fields are rejected. Automated browser QA and Do Not Track suppress client events.

Nest Mind currently uses deterministic recommendation rules, **not an autonomous external model**. It has no new payment, refund, pricing, release, deletion or customer-contact authority. Written feedback is not automatically sent to model providers.

Limitations: reach bars are unordered co-occurrence, not exact abandonment. Tokens represent browser sessions, not unique collectors, and do not measure cross-session retention. Repeated toggles/shares can inflate action counts; public analytics are not bot-proof. Raw rarity/Theme totals depend on placement and roster size. Do not infer causation or change the art roadmap from a small sample. Compare exposed views with save intent, manually review feedback, and choose one founder-approved experiment at a time.

## Build, QA and remaining risks

Implemented: six individually generated clean sources and matching avatars; deterministic premium card presentation; `/showcase`, `/albums`, `/beta`; Aurora habitat prototype; permanent card-route close-ups; landing/My Nest integration; expanded founder dashboard; additive `0006_showcase_signals` migration and tests. No changes to the canonical 369-card database or Stripe gates.

Performance: source WebP images retain 1024×1536 detail; 400×600 avatars serve previews. New images have intrinsic dimensions, responsive `sizes` and default lazy loading. No blanket preload or autoplay video. Legacy large PNGs remain preserved; existing active world/pack assets already use WebP. Remaining legacy plain-image warnings need measurement-led optimization, not deletion of source files. Real-device LCP/CLS and slow-network testing remain necessary; a successful build is not a performance score.

Accessibility: labeled buttons, focus outlines, intrinsic image space, text outside art, accessible dialog title/description, visible static/motion controls and minimum 44px new touch targets. Reduced-motion CSS overrides pointer transforms. Manual keyboard/dialog and narrow-layout verification is recorded in the release notes; a full screen-reader/contrast audit remains separate.

Automated tests verify canonical six mappings, equal stat totals, release gates, source hashes, album membership/unique progress, analytics input rejection, migration compatibility, rollback atomicity and receipt-order measurement. Existing commerce tests cover unpaid rejection, webhook signatures, duplicate/concurrent fulfillment and owner-scoped openings; they do not demonstrate live Stripe activation.

Known existing HIGH art incidents remain on #001, #007 and #008. These are preserved release blockers, despite deterministic visual overlays. AI source originality, name clearance, audience/parental policy, accessibility on actual devices, privacy retention and digital service continuity require further review. Commerce economics and legal disclosures require business/legal decisions before any future paid release.

See [deployment and verification record](showcase-release-verification.md) for exact tested commit/deployment status. No fabricated users, feedback or production analytics were created.
