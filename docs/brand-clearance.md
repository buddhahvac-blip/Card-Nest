# CardNest name clearance and ethical-use gate

Status: **HOLD FOR COUNSEL**  
Commercial name clearance: **NOT YET APPROVED**

## Why this gate exists

Current public research shows active third-party use of the exact or near-exact name **CardNest** in card collecting, trading-card retail, and adjacent card businesses. That creates a real likelihood-of-confusion risk that must be professionally evaluated before CardNest is treated as legally cleared for commercial expansion.

## Required clearance work

1. Search USPTO live and pending marks for CARDNEST, CARD NEST, phonetic equivalents, and visually similar marks.
2. Search common-law use across the web, marketplaces, social platforms, app stores, domains, state registrations, and business directories.
3. Compare goods/services and trade channels, not just exact trademark classes.
4. Identify earliest documented use dates for potentially conflicting users.
5. Review the current CardNest logo and trade dress separately from the word mark.
6. Obtain a written clearance assessment from a U.S.-licensed trademark attorney before commercial launch.
7. If cleared, file the appropriate federal trademark application for the actual goods/services CardNest offers or has a bona fide intent to offer.
8. Preserve dated evidence of first use, original art creation, source files, and launch materials.
9. Use **TM** only as an optional common-law notice while unregistered; never use **®** unless a U.S. federal registration actually issues for that mark.
10. Re-run clearance before expanding into materially new goods/services or countries.

## Ethical brand rules

- Never imply affiliation with Pokémon, Magic: The Gathering, or another CardNest business.
- Keep third-party discovery/affiliate areas visibly separate from original CardNest products.
- Do not copy another company's logo, layout, trade dress, descriptions, mascots, or marketing language.
- Do not buy confusing domains solely to divert another business's customers.
- Do not suppress or conceal a known potential conflict from customers, partners, counsel, or a trademark filing.
- If professional clearance identifies a substantial conflict, prioritize a distinctive rebrand before paid growth rather than trying to force ownership of a crowded name.
